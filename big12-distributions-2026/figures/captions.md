**fig-02-twelve-schools** (replaces the twelve-school table in "The Big 12 was never an equal-share conference")
The Big 12's twelve full-share distributions for 2024-25, ranked, on an axis that runs from $36 million to $44 million rather than from zero. The shaded band spans the lowest to the highest of the ten schools below Arizona State and Iowa State, a width of $2,070,220; the dashed line is the median of those ten. Arizona State, which won the conference and played one playoff game, sits $3,856,336 above that median, and Iowa State sits $2,041,212 above it. BYU, Cincinnati, UCF, and Houston are on entry-agreement shares and are not shown. Source: Big 12 Form 990, fiscal year ending June 30, 2025, Schedule A, Part I, via ProPublica. Band, median, and premiums calculated.

**fig-03-two-conference-spreads** (replaces both five-season spread tables, in "The pattern goes back further than the playoff" and "The correction")
Top-to-bottom spread among each conference's full-share schools in each of the last five seasons, the Big Ten and the Big 12 drawn from a common origin on one dollar axis so the two can be read against each other. The Big Ten's four years inside $375,001 are the short bars; the Big 12's widest year is 2022-23, when TCU reached the national championship game. 2024-25, the first expanded-playoff year, is in red for both. Oklahoma State and Oklahoma tied on top of the Big 12 in 2021-22. Membership changes each year and the school counts are printed beside each bar. Source: Big Ten and Big 12 Form 990 filings, fiscal years ending June 30, 2021 through June 30, 2025, Schedule A, Part I, via ProPublica. Spreads calculated.

**fig-04-correction** (replaces the premium-per-game table in "The correction")
Each Big Ten playoff school's distribution premium over $78,057,558, the median of the thirteen full-share schools that played no playoff game. The upper panel divides each premium by games played and sets it against the $3,000,000 per-game travel allowance: Indiana lands $47,661 short of the line, while Penn State and Ohio State run $621,201 and $373,631 past it, which the piece does not explain. The lower panel shows the same premiums as totals over the travel allowance blocks each school's run would generate, with a hollow marker for the prize money alone that a pass-through policy would have added, $4,000,000 for Indiana's bid and $20,000,000 for Ohio State's title run before travel. No prize figure for Penn State's three-game run is stated in the piece, so none is drawn. Oregon also played one playoff game but is on a partial share and is outside the sixteen. Source: Big Ten Form 990, fiscal year ending June 30, 2025, Schedule A, Part I, via ProPublica. Travel allowance and prize amounts are announced College Football Playoff figures; the Big Ten's equal split of prize money and pass-through of travel is as described by Forbes. Median, premiums, and per-game figures calculated; per-game figures rounded to the dollar.
