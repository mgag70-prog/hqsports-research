## Post 1 (279/280)

On October 12 Illinois denied my FOIA request for what it paid athletes by sport, calling the split a trade secret. So I checked its Big Ten neighbors. Ohio State, Michigan, Michigan State, Iowa, Indiana: none has published revenue sharing by sport in dollars. Conference policy.

## Post 2 (280/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-01-peer-table.png`

What the six have said. Ohio State: $18 million to four sports ("Numbers create narratives"). Michigan: about 75% to football. Indiana: football 70% to 75%. Illinois: "in those ranges" of 75-15-5-5, four sports over 98 percent. Michigan State, Iowa: totals only. By sport: nobody.

## Post 3 (278/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-02-category-44.png`

The difference: the NCAA added Category 44, "Institutional NIL Revenue Share," to its financial form in FY2025, by program and by sport. Illinois posts that form every January; of the six, only Ohio State also makes it public. FY2026 is the first version with money on the line.

## Post 4 (270/280)

Illinois's letter, after the denial, says the FY2026 report "will be posted on this page when they become available." If it goes up the way the last eight did, the by-sport split nobody in the Big Ten has published will be on page 74 of a PDF, around the end of January.

## Post 5 (275/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-03-revenue-expenses.png`

For scale: Illinois athletics ran a $4.9 million surplus in FY2025 on $194.8 million of revenue. Ohio State's revenue was $336.1 million, 1.7 times Illinois's; Indiana's $183.4 million. Illinois pays the same $20.5 million into revenue sharing as Ohio State at half the size.
