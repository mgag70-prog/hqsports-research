## 1. Where the talks stand (short)
- No new proposal or counter since MLB's November 3 offer (cap delayed to 2028), which the union rejected the same day (MLBTR). Link week 1 for the numbers; don't repeat them.
- 20 days until the agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1.

## 2. The money that moved last time
- November 2021: teams committed more than $1.7 billion to free agents before the December 2 lockout (CBS Sports, Dayn Perry, Dec 2 2021). Five nine-figure deals. The Rangers alone: $561.2 million (Seager 10 years $325M, Semien 7 years $175M, Jon Gray), 32.8% of the total.
- Don't state the 2021 window's length: the 2021 rules had a different opening period.

## 3. The money that has moved this time
- Free agency opened at 5 p.m. ET November 2; more than 140 players hit the market (CBS, Nov 3).
- First significant signing, per MLBTR: Emilio Pagan to the Tigers on November 10, day 9 of the 30-day window, one year at about $11 million (Heyman). He'd declined a $10 million player option with Cincinnati, so he gained about $1 million. MLBTR had predicted two years and $20 million (rank 35 of its top 50).
- Other signings so far have undisclosed terms (Weiss, Padres; Stock, Mets). Reported dollars through November 10: $11 million, 0.64% of November 2021's total. Phrase carefully: "reported" dollars.
- Cover line goes here: "20 days. $11 million."

## 4. What the market could still do
- MLBTR's top 50 predictions add up to $2.38 billion, 1.39 times November 2021. Skubal alone is predicted at nine years, $425 million: 17.8% of it. Without him, $1.96 billion. Five predicted nine-figure deals (Skubal, Rogers, Peralta, Suzuki, Arozarena), the same count as November 2021.
- Caveat: those are predictions for the whole offseason, not for 20 days. Don't present them as what will sign by December 1.
- The Dodgers and Mets are meeting with Skubal this week at the GM Meetings in Las Vegas (MLBTR headlines).
- Why teams may wait: CBS (Nov 4) quotes an official, "We're really operating under uncertainty now," and an agent, "Nothing motivates clubs more than a deadline." A cap that starts in 2028 makes long contracts signed now a bet on rules nobody has agreed to. (That last sentence is analysis; keep it as the writer's reasoning, not reporting.)
