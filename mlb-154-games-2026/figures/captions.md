**fig-01-tickets-lost** (in "What the gate loses", after the first paragraph)
Tickets each club would lose from four fewer home dates, at its own 2026 per-game attendance, all 30 clubs sorted. The Dodgers lose the most, 199,220, and the Athletics the fewest, 39,468; the league loses 3,545,372 tickets, 4.94% of the 71,752,722 it drew in 2026. Source: Baseball-Reference, 2026 attendance; the four-date figures are per-game attendance times four. Calculated.

**fig-02-lose-vs-add** (in "What the postseason adds", after the sizing paragraph)
What four lost home dates cost at the gate, against what eight added Division Series games could add back. The losses use MLB's 2024 gate per home game as Sportico reported it: the Dodgers $17.16 million, the Cubs $13.00 million, and about $2.00 million for a club at the bottom of the league. The add-back, $53.4 million to $64.1 million, is the best case for all 30 clubs combined: eight games at $6.68 million to $8.01 million each, the 2025 average across the 32 pool games if every one paid the pool 60% or if every one paid 50%, if every series went seven. Under the current split $45.4 million to $54.5 million of that reaches the clubs and none reaches the players' pool. Source: Sportico, May 30, 2025 and December 2025; MLB.com glossary, "Postseason Share"; FanGraphs, October 7, 2014. The per-game figures and the add-back are estimates.

**fig-03-postseason-split** (in "What the postseason adds", after the FanGraphs paragraph)
How postseason gate is split by game, as FanGraphs described the CBA's rules in 2014, MLB.com's glossary states the pool's shares, and Sportico described the 2025 pool. The Commissioner's Office takes 15% of every game. In the two Wild Card games, 50% goes to the players' pool and 35% to the two clubs. In the other minimum games, three in a Division Series, four in the LCS and World Series, 60% goes to the players' pool and 25% to the two clubs. In every game past the minimum, including Games 4 and 5 of a Division Series today and Games 6 and 7 under the proposal, 0% goes to the pool and 85% to the clubs. A third Wild Card game's split isn't in the sources. Attributed to those reports, not to the agreement's text. Not legal or financial advice.

**fig-04-tv-deals** (in "What pays for it", after the paragraph on the five deals)
MLB's national television deals by partner, listed annual value: Fox $728.6 million and Turner $535 million, both 2022 through 2028; ESPN about $550 million, NBC about $200 million and Netflix about $50 million, all 2026 through 2028. Together, $2,063.6 million a year. Every deal ends after 2028, and the 154-game season would begin in 2029. Apple's deal isn't verified and is left out. Source: Sportico, 2020; Axios, November 19 and 20, 2025; Boardroom, November 26, 2025.
