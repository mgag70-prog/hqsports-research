## Post 1 (274/280)

Attach: `figures/cover-fal-1200x630.png`

MLB wants to cut the season to 154 games from 2029, contingent on a salary cap. Eight fewer games is four fewer home dates per club. At 2026 attendance that's 3,545,372 fewer tickets a year, 4.94% of what the 30 clubs drew. The money that pays for the cut isn't at the gate.

## Post 2 (277/280)

Four lost home dates at each club's own 2026 average: the Dodgers lose 199,220 tickets, the Athletics 39,468. In dollars, from MLB's 2024 gate report as Sportico described it: about $17.16 million for the Dodgers, $13.00 million for the Cubs, about $2.00 million at the bottom.

## Post 3 (276/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-02-lose-vs-add.png`

The add-back is a best-of-seven Division Series, up to eight more games. The 2025 pool-game average, an estimate, is $6.68 million to $8.01 million a game: $53.4 million to $64.1 million of gate for 30 clubs if every series goes seven. The Dodgers' four dates: $17.16 million.

## Post 4 (270/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-03-postseason-split.png`

In the split FanGraphs described in 2014, games past a series' minimum fund no players' pool: 15% to the Commissioner's Office, 85% to clubs. Games 6 and 7 add two: $45.4 million to $54.5 million to clubs, $8.0 million to $9.6 million to the Commissioner, $0 to players.

## Post 5 (275/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-04-tv-deals.png`

Television pays for it. Five national deals on the record run to $2,063.6 million a year, Apple excluded, and every one expires after 2028. The 154-game season starts in 2029 with a Monday window to sell. National money is split equally among 30 clubs; gate is shared at 48%.
