## Post 1 (253/280)

Baseball has had three strikes that cost regular-season games. Per-game attendance took 2 seasons to recover from 1972, 2 from 1981, and 13 from 1994-95, the one fought over a salary cap. The owners have now proposed a cap for the first time since 1994.

## Post 2 (208/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-02-recovery.png`

Each line is indexed to the last full season before its strike. The 1972 and 1981 dips were gone within two seasons, while 1995 came in 19.2% below 1993 and attendance didn't clear that mark again until 2006.

## Post 3 (246/280)

Two caveats. 1993 was an expansion year, 26 teams to 28, and per-game attendance jumped 16.7%, so that baseline starts on a bump. Measure from the 1994 record pace instead and recovery takes 12 seasons rather than 13. Either way it lands in 2006.

## Post 4 (232/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-05-long-view.png`

Attendance peaked in 2007 at 32,696 per game, and no season since has matched it. 2026 is running at 29,460: 5.7% below the 1994 pace the strike interrupted, and 9.9% below that peak. A 2027 stoppage wouldn't be interrupting a boom.

## Post 5 (268/280)

The CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1, 2026. The record offers a lockout that cost no games (2021-22), strikes that cost some (1972 and 1981, down 3.7% and 6.8%), and one that canceled a World Series (1994-95, down 19.2% in 1995). Four cases is a thin record.
