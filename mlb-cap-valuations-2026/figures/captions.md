**fig-04-multiples** (in "What the owners asked for", after the paragraph on the gap)
Left: average team value as a multiple of revenue, as Forbes cited the four leagues on March 20, 2026: MLB 7.0x, NHL 9x, NFL 10.7x, NBA 12.9x. The NHL figure is from Forbes' December 2025 hockey list, 8.9x rounded to 9x, and the NFL figure is from its August 2025 football list; Forbes' September 9, 2026 NFL list now puts the NFL at 13.4x. Right, on the same scale: the two leagues fifteen years earlier, when Forbes put baseball at 2.5x on its March 2011 list and hockey at 2.5x on its 2012 list, as the 2014 hockey list stated it. Baseball is the cheapest of the four per dollar of revenue, and the only one without a cap. Source: Forbes, March 20, 2026; Forbes NHL lists, December 2025 and November 2014; Forbes MLB list, March 2011; Forbes NFL lists, August 28, 2025 and September 9, 2026.

**fig-03-growth** (in "The clean test", after the growth-gap sentence)
Compound annual growth in Forbes' average team value, NHL and MLB, over the five lists before the 2005 cap and the seven after it. From the 1999 lists to the 2004 lists, hockey grew 3.84% a year and baseball 6.02%, a gap of 2.18 points. From the 2004 lists to the 2011 lists, hockey grew 5.68% a year and baseball 8.52%, a gap of 2.84 points. Both leagues grew faster after 2004; baseball pulled further ahead. Source: Forbes average team values by list year, from the data file. Growth rates calculated.

**fig-01-indexed** (in "The clean test", after the growth-gap sentence and fig-03)
Forbes' average team value for each league, divided by its 2004 list value, so both start at 100. The red rule marks the 2005 cap; there is no hockey point for 2005 because there was no season. Left, the full run to the 2025 and 2026 lists, where hockey's index reaches 1,350 and baseball's 983. Right, the same series from 1999 to 2012 at a scale where the seven years after the cap can be read: baseball's line is above hockey's the whole way. Hollow markers are averages computed from team tables or taken from a secondary report; the data file flags each. Source: Forbes average team values by list year, from the data file. Index calculated.

**fig-02-ratio** (in "When hockey did catch up", after the first paragraph)
The average baseball team's value divided by the average hockey team's, by Forbes list year. It was 1.81 on the 2004 lists, 2.18 on the 2011 lists, 2.83 at the peak on the 2020 lists, and 1.18 on the 2025 lists. The marks are the 2005 cap, the Rogers deal announced in November 2013, the ESPN and Turner deals of 2021, and Diamond Sports' Chapter 11 filing in March 2023. The dip to 1.66 in 2014 and the rebound to 2.38 in 2015 are mostly Forbes changing its method for the two leagues in different years. Source: Forbes average team values by list year, from the data file. Ratio calculated.
