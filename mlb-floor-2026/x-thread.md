## Post 1

Attach: `figures/cover-fal-1200x630.png`

On May 28 the owners proposed a $245.3 million cap and a $171.2 million floor. Lay those lines over 2026 payrolls and the Brewers, baseball's best team at 103-59, were $16.5 million under the floor. So were the Rays, AL champions in six. Both pennant winners, under the floor.

## Post 2

Attach: `figures/fig-01-final-four.png`

The final four: Dodgers $433.06 million, $187.8 million over the cap. Brewers $154.71 million, $16.5 million under the floor. Rays $113.23 million, $58.0 million under. Guardians $92.58 million, $78.6 million under. The Dodgers outspent the other three combined by $72.5 million.

## Post 3

Attach: `figures/fig-02-all-30.png`

All 30 clubs: nine over the cap, twelve under the floor, nine between. Moving every club inside the lines cuts $627.3 million and adds $548.3 million, so players are paid $79.0 million less. MLB says players gain $39 million. The union says they lose more than $500 million.

## Post 4

Attach: `figures/fig-03-wins-vs-payroll.png`

The fair part: over-cap clubs averaged 88.3 wins, under-floor clubs 80.0. Money still buys wins on average, and the Mets ($359.48 million, 74-88) show it isn't automatic. But the best record (Brewers, 103-59) and the third-best (Rays, 98-64) were both under the floor.

## Post 5

Attach: `figures/fig-04-change-to-comply.png`

A floor is a spending order for the cheapest clubs. The four cheapest, the Rays, Cardinals, Guardians and Marlins, would have had to add $58.0 million, $62.4 million, $78.6 million and $86.8 million. Two of the four played for a pennant. The cap side cuts more: $627.3 million.
