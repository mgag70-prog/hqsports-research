**fig-01-club-grid** (in "The question on the invoice", after the second paragraph)
All 30 clubs, one tile each, by what their public season-ticket or ticket terms say about canceled games, as read on October 8, 2026. Four name a lockout or strike: the Cubs, Reds, Diamondbacks and Yankees. Seven promise a credit or refund for canceled games without naming a cause. Ten leave the remedy to the club or the league, seven say sales are final or have no canceled-game clause on the public page, and two, the Mets and Athletics, have no public season-ticket terms I could find. Source: each club's posted terms, listed by URL in data/club-terms.csv. Classification is mine.

**fig-02-lockout-table** (in "The four that name a lockout", after the Yankees paragraph)
The four clauses that mention a lockout, best to worst for the fan. The Reds cut fees pro rata and refund on written request within 60 days of the league confirming the cancellation; the Diamondbacks give a credit toward future games and no refund; the Cubs keep the money and apply it to the next season if fewer than 41 home games are played, and owe nothing for canceled games if 41 or more are; the Yankees disclaim liability for force majeure and state no remedy. Quotes are verbatim from each club's posted terms, October 8, 2026, shortened to the operative words. Not legal advice.

**fig-03-refund-clocks** (in "Who owes the deadline", after the paragraph)
Every stated deadline in the 30 clubs' public terms, and who it runs against. The Angels and Giants give the fan 30 days from the cancellation to ask for a refund; at the Angels a missed window means the refund is "forever waived." The Reds give themselves 60 days to pay. The Blue Jays' general ticket terms refund "in as soon as" 60 days from the game date, which is a floor, not a ceiling. The other 26 clubs state no payment clock. Source: data/club-terms.csv, read October 8, 2026.

**fig-04-cubs-line** (in "The Cubs' 41-game line", after the 154-game paragraph)
The Cubs' terms turn on whether 41 home games are played. On the 81-game home schedule, that leaves 40 games that can be canceled with the season still counted as full and no refund owed for them; below 41, the club keeps the fees and applies them to the next season. On the 77-game home schedule MLB proposed on October 8 for 2029, the same line allows 36. The 41-game threshold is from the Cubs' posted terms; the 40 and 36 are arithmetic. Not legal advice.
