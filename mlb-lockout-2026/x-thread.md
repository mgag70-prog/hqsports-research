The CBA expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1, and 2027 renewal invoices are already out. I read all 30 MLB clubs' public season-ticket terms to see what happens to the money if a lockout cancels games. Four mention a lockout at all. One promises cash back on a clock.

## Post 2 (271/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-02-lockout-table.png`

The four that name a lockout, best to worst for the fan. Reds: fees cut pro rata, refund within 60 days on written request. Diamondbacks: credit only. Cubs: payments stay due; under 41 home games the club keeps the money for next season. Yankees: no liability, no remedy.

## Post 3 (276/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-04-cubs-line.png`

The Cubs' terms turn on 41 home games. On an 81-game home schedule, 40 games can be canceled with the season still counted as full and no refund owed. Under MLB's October 8 proposal, 154 games from 2029 and 77 home dates, the same line allows 36. My reading, not legal advice.

## Post 4 (275/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-01-club-grid.png`

Seven clubs promise a credit or refund for canceled games without naming a cause: Brewers, Royals, Giants, Angels, Phillies, Braves, Blue Jays. Ten leave it to the club or league. Seven say sales are final or have no clause. Two, the Mets and Athletics, have no public terms.

## Post 5 (278/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-03-refund-clocks.png`

Where a deadline exists, it runs against the fan more often than the club. Angels and Giants: 30 days to ask; at the Angels a missed window means the refund is "forever waived." Reds: the club pays within 60 days. Blue Jays: "in as soon as" 60 days. The other 26 state no clock.
