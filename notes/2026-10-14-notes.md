## Wednesday, October 14: the add-back doesn't add up

Attach: `mlb-154-games-2026/figures/fig-02-lose-vs-add.png`

MLB's 154-game plan takes four home dates from every club and gives back a best-of-seven Division Series, up to 8 more postseason games a year. Best case, every series going seven, those 8 games are $53.4M to $64.1M of gate for all 30 clubs together, and under the current split none of it reaches the players' pool. The Dodgers alone lose about $17.16M of gate from their four dates. The whole league's add-back is three or four Dodgers. The gate isn't what pays for the cut.
https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/eight-fewer-games-is-35-million-fewer

## Friday, October 16: the last year before revenue sharing

Attach: nothing. No link.

Illinois athletics' own numbers for fiscal 2025, the last year before it started paying athletes: $194.8M of revenue and a $4.9M surplus. Football made $38.7M, men's basketball $16.7M, and the other sports lost $25.4M before the department's overhead. That's the cushion. Revenue sharing for a Big Ten school runs up to about $20M a year under the settlement. Where does about $20M a year come from?

## Saturday, October 17: 2021 called

Attach: nothing.

The 154-game idea isn't new. In February 2021 MLB offered the players a 154-game season with full pay and an expanded postseason, and the union said no within days, in part because it was being asked to accept expanded playoffs it had already turned down (CBS Sports, February 2, 2021). Fewer games, full pay, more October: the same trade as this week's proposal. What's different now is the price tag attached to it, a salary cap.
