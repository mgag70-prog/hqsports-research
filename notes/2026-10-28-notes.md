## Thursday, October 29: the contracts nobody has explained

Attach: `mlb-2027-cap-2026/figures/fig-04-over-265.png`

The player side of the cap. The owners' plan would start a free agent changing teams at 15% of the cap, $36.795 million a year. Eight players already count more than that in 2027, Kyle Tucker and Juan Soto among them. And 18 contracts still running are bigger than $265 million, the largest the proposal would allow. Nobody on either side has said what happens to them, and the agreement expires December 1.

## Friday, October 30: stadium teaser (PROVISIONAL: recheck against the final stadium draft)

Elected officials almost always say yes to a stadium. Voters don't. J.C. Bradbury, writing in The Conversation in September, puts it at 96% of proposals approved when councils and legislatures decide, against only 58% of referendums. I've gathered what recent stadium and arena deals cost the public, and who got a vote on each. That piece is out this weekend.

## Sunday, November 1: Kansas City (PROVISIONAL: recheck against the final stadium draft)

Attach: nothing.

Kansas City shows what happens after voters say no. In April of 2024, Jackson County turned down a sales tax for the Chiefs and Royals, 58% to 42%. Kansas then stepped in for the Chiefs: about $1.8 billion in STAR bonds, roughly 60% of a stadium expected to cost around $3 billion, just across the state line. The vote didn't stop the subsidy. It moved the decision to a government those voters didn't get to ask.
