## Tuesday, November 3: who gets a vote

Attach: `stadium-subsidies-2026/figures/fig-01-the-bill.png`

The people who decide on a stadium are rarely the people who pay for it. J.C. Bradbury, writing in The Conversation, puts elected bodies at 96% of stadium proposals approved, against 58% of referendums passed. Of the 38 deals in my piece, three went to a public vote and all three passed, the Thunder's arena among them, and it's 94.4% public. A referendum guarantees taxpayers get asked; it doesn't guarantee they say no.
https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/taxpayers-have-committed-135-billion

## Thursday, November 5: who keeps the gains

Attach: nothing.

Set the public money for a stadium against what the team is worth and the owners' side of the deal gets hard to miss. The public contribution to the Royals' ballpark, about $1.14 billion, is 69.5% of the team's Forbes value. Tampa's $876 million for the Rays is 51.5% of theirs. Forbes values are estimates, not sale prices, but the split holds either way: the owner keeps the appreciation, and the public keeps the bonds.
https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/taxpayers-have-committed-135-billion

### Friday, November 6: scarcity piece is out

Attach: `scarcity-premium-2026/figures/cover-fal-1080x1080.png`

New piece: does an elite team sell for more than Forbes says it's worth? I checked 33 control sales since 2012. The median buyer paid 24.0% more, and 28 of the 33 cleared the estimate. The biggest premiums didn't go to the trophy teams, though. The Hornets drew 76.5%. The Celtics drew 1.7%, the Mets 0.8%, and the Mavericks sold 22.2% below Forbes.

### Saturday, November 7: the floor's 90% rule

Attach: `nothing`

The owners' new salary floor has some give: a club could spend 90% of it in a year, $162.18 million, if it meets a multi-year minimum. It rescues nobody on this year's payrolls, because all 12 clubs under the floor were below that line too. It does cut what they'd need to add, from $656.3 million to $440.0 million.
https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/26-days-left-the-owners-moved-their

### Sunday, November 8: the calendar premium

Attach: `scarcity-premium-2026/figures/fig-03-by-period.png`

Part of what looks like a premium over Forbes is just the calendar. In my set of 33 team sales, deals measured against a Forbes list more than six months old ran a median 25.0% over the estimate. Against fresher lists, 17.7%. And Forbes isn't usually far off: 8 of the 33 sales landed within 3% of its number.
