**fig-03-savings-split** (new, in "Where the savings come from now", after the paragraph on the tie)
Extension savings split by source, present value to 2026, at the three readings of true talent in the table: the September 14 base case at 6.5, the two-season average at 8.0, and 2026 at face value at 10.6. The navy bars are the four control years, 2027 through 2030, net of the 2027 loss and the $5 million signing bonus; the red bars are the two free agent seasons the extension bought out, 2031 and 2032. At 6.5 the control years carry most of the savings, $19.1 million against $9.4 million. At 8.0 the two are tied at $34.0 million each, and at 10.6 the free agent years lead, $76.5 million to $59.9 million, because a better player raises the 2031 free agent counterfactual more than it raises his arbitration awards. Every other input is the September 14 model unchanged: $9.0 million per win, 100% free agent capture. Source: model in pca-extension-2026, run September 28, 2026, through pca-mvp-2026/model/recompute_outline.py. Savings calculated.

**fig-04-war-scales** (new, in "What would make this wrong", after the paragraph on the WAR scale)
His wins above replacement by season, 2024 through 2026, on each site's own scale, drawn as two separate lines and never averaged together. FanGraphs, the scale used throughout this piece, has him at 2.6, 5.4 and 10.6; Baseball Reference has 2.3, 5.9 and 9.8. FanGraphs rates his 2025 lower than Baseball Reference does, which is why his 2025 lands on the model's 5.397 breakeven on one scale and clears it by half a win on the other, and rates his 2026 higher. Source: FanGraphs, final, read September 28, 2026; Baseball Reference, the page updated September 27, 2026, read September 28, 2026.
