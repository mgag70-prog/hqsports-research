## Post 1

Attach: `figures/fig-01-premium-by-deal.png`

I checked 33 control sales of major-league teams against the Forbes value when each was announced. The median buyer paid 24.0% more. 28 sold above Forbes and 5 below. In all: $89.56 billion paid against $74.07 billion of Forbes value.

## Post 2

If scarcity set the price, the rarest teams should clear the estimate by the most. The Celtics went 1.7% over at $6.1 billion, the Mets 0.8%, the Commanders 8.0%. The Mavericks sold 22.2% below Forbes.

## Post 3

The top of the table: Hornets 76.5%, Devils 56.1%, Bills 49.7%, Suns 48.1%. The Padres' 74.7% included $200 million of TV money, so call it 31.0%. To be fair to the scarcity case, the Lakers (40.8%) and Seahawks (43.5%) did clear it easily.

## Post 4

Attach: `figures/fig-02-by-league.png`

By league: NHL 36.5% on 7 sales, NBA 25.0% on 13, MLB 16.7% on 7, NFL 16.0% on 6. Those are small samples, and one deal can move a median built on six or seven. Deals of $3 billion or more: 24.5%. Below that: 18.7%.

## Post 5

Attach: `figures/fig-03-by-period.png`

Timing matters too: 34.5% from 2012 to 2017, 7.1% from 2018 to 2022, 20.0% from 2023 to 2026. And sales measured against a list more than six months old ran 25.0%, against 17.7% for fresher ones.

## Post 6

Forbes values are estimates, not prices, and the teams that sell are a selected group. Bundled arenas inflate some premiums. Fourteen deals are out, each named with the reason. On disputed prices the median runs from 20.0% to 25.0%.
