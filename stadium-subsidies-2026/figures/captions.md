**fig-01-the-bill** (in "The bill", after the second paragraph)
All 38 projects in the set, $49.5 billion of building, sorted by public dollars. Public money totals $18.3 billion, 37.1% of the cost; eight projects were privately financed, and among the 30 that took public money the median public share is 60.0%. The Thunder's arena, at 94.4%, is the highest. Public means state, county and city construction money as reported, not interest, maintenance, lost property tax or land. Nominal dollars. Source: data/projects.csv, one cited report per project, with the headline deals checked a second time against independent reporting.

**fig-02-share-rising** (in "The newest deals ask the public for more", after the second paragraph)
Public share of each new venue's cost: the median public share is 37.8% for the 14 that opened from 2015 through 2026 and 60.0% for the nine under construction or approved, which open from 2027 to 2032. Renovations are left out. Nine is a small sample, and one deal moving would shift the median. Source: data/projects.csv.

**fig-03-team-values** (in "Who keeps the gains", after the first paragraph)
Public money for each subsidized team's venue as a share of what Forbes says the team is worth, from the Royals at 69.5% and the Rays at 51.5% down to the Raiders at 7.3%. Baseball teams are worth less, so the same public check is a far bigger share. Forbes values are estimates, not sale prices: NFL list September 9, 2026; NBA, October 16, 2026; MLB, March 20, 2026. For Capital One Arena the comparison uses the Wizards' value only. Source: data/team-values.csv and data/projects.csv.

**fig-04-open-asks** (in "What's still being asked", at the top)
Ten asks from data/asks.csv that failed or are still pending, with what each sought and what happened. The Bears have three rows: the shelved lakefront plan, Indiana's Hammond offer, whose figure is a state estimate of district tax revenue rather than an appropriation, and an Illinois stadium authority that Crain's reported on October 29, 2026 was nearing a deal, with nothing announced. The Broncos' public infrastructure sits next to a stadium the team says is privately funded. Source: data/asks.csv, each row with its cited report.
