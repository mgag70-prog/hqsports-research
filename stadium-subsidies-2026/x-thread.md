## Post 1

Attach: `figures/fig-01-the-bill.png`

I went through 38 recent major-league stadium and arena deals. Together they cost $49.5 billion, and state, county and city governments put in $18.3 billion of it. Among the 30 that took public money, the median public share is 60.0%.

## Post 2

Attach: `figures/fig-02-share-rising.png`

The newest deals ask for more. New venues that opened from 2015 through 2026: median 37.8% public. The nine new venues under construction or approved: 60.0%. Small sample, but the biggest checks point the same way.

## Post 3

The headline number leaves things out. Nevada's $750 million for the Raiders becomes about $1.35 billion with interest. Tax-exempt bonds for 57 stadiums cost the Treasury $4.3 billion. Lost property tax: about $18 billion in present value.

## Post 4

Of the 38 deals, three had a public vote, and all three passed. The Thunder's arena is 94.4% public, the highest in the set, and Oklahoma City voters approved it with 71% of the vote. A referendum guarantees taxpayers get asked, not that they say no.

## Post 5

Attach: `figures/fig-03-team-values.png`

Against Forbes's team values, which are estimates: the public's $876 million for the Rays is 51.5% of what the team is worth, and the Royals' is 69.5%. The owner keeps the appreciation, and the public keeps the bonds.
