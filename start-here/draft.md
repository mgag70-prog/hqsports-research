# Start here

*What Ledger & Whistle is, when it publishes, and five pieces to read first.*

---

Ledger & Whistle covers the business of sports, by the numbers. Every piece starts with a dataset I build from public sources and check line by line, and the data and the scripts that check it are [public on GitHub](https://github.com/mgag70-prog/hqsports-research), so you can rerun anything you doubt. Every piece also ends with a prediction on the record, and I grade those in public when the answer comes in.

## Five to read first

1. [The median buyer paid 24% more than Forbes said the team was worth.](https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/the-median-buyer-paid-24-more-than) Across 33 control sales since 2012, the Hornets drew 76.5% and the Celtics 1.7%, and the trophy teams weren't the ones that drew the biggest premiums.
2. [Taxpayers have committed $13.5 billion to 19 stadiums and arenas that haven't opened yet.](https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/taxpayers-have-committed-135-billion) Across 38 deals the public put in $18.3 billion, and the ones still being built ask for more of the bill, not less.
3. [I paid for the same four Wrigley seats for 18 years.](https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/i-paid-for-the-same-four-wrigley) This is the personal one, built from my own invoices. After inflation the price rose 13.0% in the first 11 years and 44.9% in the last six.
4. [Pete Crow-Armstrong was paid $894,000 to win the MVP.](https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/pete-crow-armstrong-was-paid-894000) The bonus comes out of a league pool that both sides of the lockout fight are arguing over.
5. [13 days left, and MLB took its salary-cap case to fans.](https://ledgerandwhistle.substack.com/p/13-days-left-and-mlb-took-its-salary) It's part of the weekly countdown to the lockout. On 2026 payrolls, the top club spent 5.13 times the bottom one.
