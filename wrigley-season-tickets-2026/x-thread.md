## Post 1 (222/280)

Attach: `figures/cover-1200x630.png`

I paid for the same four Wrigley seats for 18 years and kept every invoice. After inflation, the price rose 13.0% in the first 11 and 44.9% in the last six. Inflation ran at the same pace in both periods. The price didn't.

## Post 2 (276/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-02-real-total.png`

In 2021 dollars, the four seats went from $23,669 in 2004 to $26,750 in 2015, 1.1% a year while CPI ran 2.1%. Then $26,750 to $38,751 by 2021, 6.4% a year while CPI ran 2.3%. Same seats, same section. The early years are rounded totals, so I read them from the endpoints only.

## Post 3 (277/280)

Attach: `figures/fig-03-price-vs-cpi.png`

From 2016 on the invoices are exact, and every year beat inflation: 15.1%, 8.1%, 8.4%, 4.9%, 8.2%, 8.3%. The Cubs announced an average increase of 10.4% for 2016 after the 97-win season. Mine was 15.1%. My ticket stub says Field Box Outfield; ABC7 gave no figure for that tier.

## Post 4 (276/280)

2020: billed $35,790, up 8.2%, for a season played without fans. Refunded, eventually. The first batch came within 30 days, which matched the Cubs' published terms. The rest took many months. I gave up the seats after 2021, 24 years in, having paid $491,330.80 before refunds.

## Post 5 (274/280)

Why this touches the lockout: season tickets are local revenue. The CBA sizes revenue sharing as what a 48% straight pool of net local revenue would move between clubs. The owners' May 28, 2026 proposal scraps that plan and pools local media money. Ticket money stays local.
